## Module 1
Below is the consolidated Module 1-specific list, drawn from the G1–G4 critical review findings. Module 1 is generally strong as an entry module:
it uses the water-point story, practical HRBA questions, low-stakes checks, self-assessment, and a first action commitment. The main fixes are
around HRBA standards grounding, duty-bearer clarity, modal/reveal overload, safe reflection, alt text, mobile/keyboard accessibility,
and completion/progress QA.
Part 1 — Module 1 Overall Issues, Findings, and Recommended Fixes
## Issues, Findings Recommended Fixes / Enhancements / Actions
Module 1 is strong as an orientation and first HRBA
mindset-shift module, but not yet final-ready without targeted
P1/P2 fixes.
Keep the structure, water-point story, knowledge check, self-assessment,
and commitment flow; implement content, accessibility, and QA fixes
before wider learner testing.
HRBA is presented practically, but the standards/principles
grounding is not explicit enough.
Add one plain-language sentence: “HRBA also uses human rights
standards and principles as a practical reference for fair decisions and
accountability.”
The module risks teaching HRBA as “good reflective
programming” rather than a standards-based accountability
approach.
Strengthen M1-S1-06 and M1-S2-01 with short wording on rights,
standards, duty-bearers, responsibilities, participation, and accountability.
State/public duty-bearer role needs clearer emphasis. Add simple wording that public authorities and mandated institutions are
primary duty-bearers, while CSOs support participation, evidence,
dialogue, and accountability.

Local duty-bearer examples are too generic. Add safe Ethiopian/local examples: kebele water committee, woreda water
office, school management committee, health facility management, local
council, service provider.
Water-point scenario is strong but can be more locally grounded. Add one or two likely responsible actors and a safe CSO facilitation role
without naming real places or disputes.
Safe advocacy/accountability framing is not yet introduced early
enough.
Introduce a light formula: verify evidence, assess risk, protect people,
engage constructively, document follow-up.
Safeguarding and do-no-harm are present but need stronger
recurrence in learner-input screens.
Add “do not disclose sensitive details” guidance before self-assessment
and action-planning fields.
Reflection and portfolio prompts may invite sensitive real
examples.
Tell learners to use fictional, generalized, or non-sensitive examples and
avoid names, identifiers, survivors, children, officials, organizations,
locations, disputes, or sensitive incidents.
Module 1 includes several reveal/modal-heavy screens. Flatten essential learning into visible summaries; use modals only for
optional detail or examples.
Core learning is sometimes hidden behind repeated clicks. Add visible “core idea” text on the main screen before any reveal/modal
interaction.
M1-S1-06, M1-S1-06A, and M1-S1-06B form a dense
conceptual cluster.
Add transition copy and reduce perceived repetition; consider merging or
tightening overlapping content.

M1-S1-08 and M1-S2-03 both teach related mindset shifts. Clarify their distinct purpose, or make one a worked example of the other.
Learning journey map adds interaction burden early in the
module.
Keep the map but simplify exploration instructions and reduce gating
friction.
First action commitment is useful but could be more concrete
and reusable.
Structure it as: “The group I may be missing is... Before I act, I will ask...
One accountability step I will add is...”
Learner persona fit is broad but not role-specific enough. Add optional role lens prompts for field staff, program managers, MEAL
staff, and advocacy/accountability staff.
Visual identity is strong, but early screens must protect trust and
clarity.
Keep the calm visual system; improve task clarity, progress cues, alt text,
and mobile usability.
Some icons and pathway visuals have empty or weak alt text. Fix all meaningful alt text; mark decorative icons correctly where visible
labels already carry the meaning.
Sector icons and shift pathway icons need accessibility
metadata.
Add alt text such as “Icon for education rights questions” or a full pathway
description for services-to-rights visuals.
Disabled Continue states may be understandable but need
clearer “what remains” cues.
Add micro-copy such as “2 of 4 explored — open two more clues to
continue.”

Mobile/tablet behavior is not sufficiently evidenced. Test all Module 1 interactive screens on mobile/tablet, especially journey
map, clue investigation, ripple interaction, responsibility map, forms, and
knowledge check.
Keyboard and focus behavior cannot be confirmed from
screenshots.
Conduct keyboard-only testing for cards, modals, buttons, matching, quiz
options, forms, and completion screens.

Add/verify screen-level accessibility metadata and test all accessibility
