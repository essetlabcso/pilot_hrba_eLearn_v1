# Batch 2A Practice Instruction Pattern

Use this pattern for complex practice screens where learners need to apply HRBA judgment, not just read content.

## Standard Instruction Blocks

### What to look at

Name the evidence or situation learners should examine.

Reusable wording:

> Look at the situation, the people affected, the barrier, and who has responsibility or influence.

### What to decide

State the decision in one sentence.

Reusable wording:

> Decide which option best protects rights-holders, uses evidence responsibly, and keeps the CSO role clear.

### How to complete the task

Make the task mechanics explicit.

Reusable wording:

> Review the worked example first. Then choose one option, check the feedback, and use the rationale before moving to the next item.

### What makes a strong answer

Define quality criteria.

Reusable wording:

> A strong answer identifies the barrier, considers risk, respects duty-bearer responsibility, supports safe participation or accountability, and explains follow-up.

### Safety reminder

Keep safe practice visible but concise.

Reusable wording:

> Use a fictional, generalized, or non-sensitive example. Do not include names, exact locations, real complaints, survivor details, children details, officials names, confidential documents, or organizational disputes.

### Continue/completion rule

Explain what unlocks the next step.

Reusable wording:

> Complete the required choice or reflection and review the feedback to continue.

## Reusable Wording Patterns By Task Type

### Matching/classification tasks

- What to look at: "Look at what the item can show and what it cannot show."
- What to decide: "Match the item to the strongest HRBA category."
- Strong answer cue: "The strongest match explains the right, barrier, responsibility, or evidence type without overclaiming."
- Completion rule: "Match all required items and review the rationale feedback."

### Actor mapping

- What to look at: "Identify rights-holders, formal duty-bearers, influence actors, and the CSO support role."
- What to decide: "Place each actor in the role that best fits their responsibility or influence."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong map keeps state institutions as primary duty-bearers and keeps the CSO role bounded."
- Completion rule: "Open or complete the required actor roles and review the feedback."

### Root-cause/capacity-gap analysis

- What to look at: "Start with the visible symptom, then look for barriers, system constraints, capacity gaps, and participation risks."
- What to decide: "Choose the cause or gap that the project can address safely and realistically."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong answer avoids blaming rights-holders and identifies barriers, responsibilities, and access conditions."
- Completion rule: "Complete each branch or required diagnosis before continuing."

### Objective repair

- What to look at: "Compare the activity-based objective with the rights-based purpose."
- What to decide: "Repair the objective so it names the barrier, rights-holder concern, responsible actor or system, participation, and accountability."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong objective does not promise what the CSO cannot control."
- Completion rule: "Review the repaired example and complete the objective choice or rewrite."

### Activity package repair

- What to look at: "Check whether each activity responds to a barrier and has a safe follow-up route."
- What to decide: "Choose the activity package that combines access, participation, actor engagement, and accountability."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong package is more than training; it removes barriers and supports responsible response."
- Completion rule: "Repair the required package and review feedback."

### Accountability/feedback loop repair

- What to look at: "Find where the loop breaks: clarity, safe use, review, response, adaptation, or follow-up."
- What to decide: "Choose the repair that closes the loop without exposing people."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong repair verifies evidence, assesses risk, protects rights-holders, engages constructively, and documents follow-up."
- Completion rule: "Repair each required loop break and review the response."

### MEAL indicator repair

- What to look at: "Check whether the indicator measures only activity or also access, participation, accountability, change, and safety."
- What to decide: "Choose a small balanced set of indicators."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong indicator set uses minimum necessary data and shows whether barriers are changing."
- Completion rule: "Repair the required indicator set and review the rationale."

### Disaggregation/data safety decision

- What to look at: "Check whether the data detail is necessary and whether combined categories could identify someone."
- What to decide: "Choose the least detailed option that still supports safe learning."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong answer aggregates or removes risky detail when small numbers could identify people."
- Completion rule: "Choose a safe data option and review the feedback."

### Reporting/storytelling decision

- What to look at: "Look for identifying details, unsupported claims, pressure to disclose, and safer evidence alternatives."
- What to decide: "Choose the report or story response that tells the truth safely."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong answer protects people, avoids blame without evidence, and offers anonymized or aggregated alternatives."
- Completion rule: "Choose or repair the claim and review why it is safer."

### Portfolio/action-plan task

- What to look at: "Choose one generalized issue, one barrier, one safe evidence source, one feasible action, and one follow-up moment."
- What to decide: "Write a practical action that can be tested safely."
- Strong answer cue: "A strong portfolio entry is specific enough to guide practice and general enough to protect people."
- Completion rule: "Enter the required safe response, save or confirm if available, and review the summary."
