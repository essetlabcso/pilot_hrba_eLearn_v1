Modal 1 title: **Really? What are child rights?**

**Child rights are a set of global legal commitments that say that all children everywhere have the right to:**
- **survive and develop**
- **be protected from violence, abuse and exploitation**
- **have their views respected**
- **have actions concerning them be taken in their best interests**

**Child rights refer to the full range of civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights for all children without discrimination. These rights are interdependent and indivisible.**

Case modal title: **Case description**

**An estimated 2.7 million children work in Egypt, the majority in agriculture, with more than 1 million hired each year for the cotton harvest, working long hours in 40C degrees.**
- **A majority of the children working for cotton co-operatives are between the ages of seven and twelve.**
- **They earn on average about one U.S. dollar each day.**
- **In a recent UNICEF survey, nearly all children interviewed reported beatings by foremen in the fields.**
- **Often children’s job involves handling plants drenched in pesticides.**
- **Many children return to work after pesticide spraying in a much shorter interval than recommended.**
- **Often children who work these fields complain of breathing difficulties at the height of summer.**

**While the Egypt Child Law of 1996 bans the employment of children under 14, it remains largely unenforced and does not address the root causes propelling youngsters into this line of work.**

**Egypt's cotton exports are worth £150m, a business that should be securing the livelihoods of the farmers. However, cotton prices have plunged, and the cost of starting each year’s crop has jumped. Many farmers’ children must work in their family fields instead of going to school. According to the UN 2005 Egypt Common Country Assessment, almost 17 per cent of Egypt's 77.5 million people were living below the poverty line in 2007, with the worst poverty being in rural areas where the cotton fields lie.**
