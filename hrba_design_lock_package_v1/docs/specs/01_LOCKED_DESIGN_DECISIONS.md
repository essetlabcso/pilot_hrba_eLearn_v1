# 01 Locked Design Decisions

## Decision 1 — Use the Two-Layer Architecture
The HRBA course will use a two-layer design:

- Layer 1: CSO Learning Hub platform / LMS layer.
- Layer 2: focused HRBA course-player layer.

This is now locked and should not be redesigned into a single long webpage or generic LMS content page.

## Decision 2 — Preserve Agora-Inspired Design Strengths
The course will preserve these Agora-inspired strengths:

- platform-to-player launch flow;
- module launch wrapper/fallback pattern;
- separate focused player window/tab or focused player shell;
- split-screen module cover;
- fixed top player header;
- left toolbar with visible labels;
- progress strip/counter;
- help overlay;
- accessibility modal;
- glossary and resources;
- captions/transcripts;
- video screen pattern;
- learning outcomes screen;
- clickable cards;
- modal explanations;
- practice checks and feedback;
- summary tabs;
- completion screen;
- return-to-platform and saved progress state.

## Decision 3 — Do Not Force Agora Screen Counts
The Agora Introduction and Module 1 examples are references, not templates for fixed HRBA screen counts.

Locked rule:

> HRBA screen count follows learning purpose, content complexity, story/scenario need, interaction design, tool activity, assessment logic, and safety/accessibility requirements.

## Decision 4 — HRBA Workbooks and Block Logic Remain the Instructional Source
Agora controls the platform/player interaction model and visual inspiration. The HRBA production workbooks, block library, course-player specification, visual asset system, safety rules, and CSO capacity logic control HRBA content and instructional design.

## Decision 5 — Adult-Learning and CSO Practice Orientation
The course must be practical, locally grounded, and action-oriented. Learning objectives, examples, activities, cases, and assessments must link to CSO practice, not only theory.

## Decision 6 — Safety and Accessibility Are Design Requirements
Every player screen, interaction, visual, case, scenario, worksheet, and learner input must be reviewed for accessibility and data-safety risk.

## Decision 7 — Module 1 Will Be the Flagship Prototype
Before scaling to all modules, Module 1 will be audited, repaired, built, and QA-approved as the production standard.
