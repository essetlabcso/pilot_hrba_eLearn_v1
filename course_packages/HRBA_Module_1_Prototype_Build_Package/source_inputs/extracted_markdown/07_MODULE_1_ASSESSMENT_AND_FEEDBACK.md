# Module 1 Assessment and Feedback Map

## Module assessment rule

All Module 1 checks are **formative only**. They help participants review, practice, reflect, and prepare for later learning.

Module 1 checks do not issue a certificate, unlock a certificate, replace the final course test, rank participants, rank CSOs, require sensitive information, or require document uploads.

Certificate eligibility remains linked to the **final course test with a score of 80% or above**.

## Assessment sequence

| Assessment ID | Placement | Assessment type | Purpose | Required? | Certificate effect |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| A1 | Section 1 | Orientation readiness check | Confirm safety, privacy, and course mechanics | Yes | No |
| A2 | Section 3 | Scenario decision | Practice asking rights-based questions before acting | Yes | No |
| A3 | Section 4 | Sorting activity | Distinguish charity, needs-based, service-delivery, and rights-based framing | Yes | No |
| A4 | Section 5 | Matching activity | Identify rights-holders, duty-bearers, CSO role, and stakeholders | Yes | No |
| A5 | Section 6 | HRBA self-assessment | Identify private HRBA starting point and priority areas | Yes | No |
| A6 | Section 7 | Module 1 quiz | Review key concepts at the end of the module | Yes | No |

## Feedback rules

Use:

- “That’s right.”
- “Good choice.”
- “Not quite.”

Do not use:

- “Incorrect”
- “Wrong”
- “Failed”
- “Pass/fail”
- “Your CSO is weak”
- “Your organization is non-compliant”
- “This affects your certificate”

## A2 — Scenario Decision

Question: Which set of questions is the strongest rights-based starting point?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | How many people can we invite, and how quickly can we finish the event? | Not quite. Numbers and timelines matter, but they do not show whether the process is inclusive, rights-aware, or accountable. |
| B | Which groups are affected, who may be excluded, what rights are connected, and who has responsibility to respond? | That’s right. These questions help the CSO identify affected groups, possible exclusion, relevant rights, and responsible actors before acting. |
| C | Which donor indicators can we report most easily? | Not quite. Donor reporting matters, but HRBA starts with people, rights, participation, and accountability. |
| D | Which participants are most likely to agree with our existing plan? | Not quite. HRBA does not only listen to people who already agree. It asks who is affected and who may be excluded. |

Best response: B

## A6 — Module 1 Quiz

### Opening text

This short quiz helps you review your learning. It is formative and does not affect your certificate.

### Question 1

What is one important HRBA shift introduced in this module?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | From communities as rights-holders to communities as passive recipients | Not quite. HRBA moves in the opposite direction: from seeing people only as recipients toward recognizing them as rights-holders. |
| B | From beneficiaries to rights-holders | That’s right. A core HRBA shift is recognizing people as rights-holders with dignity, voice, and legitimate claims. |
| C | From accountability to activity reporting only | Not quite. HRBA strengthens accountability rather than reducing work to activity reporting. |
| D | From participation to decisions made only by the CSO | Not quite. HRBA strengthens meaningful participation in decisions that affect people. |

Best response: B

### Question 2

Which question is most rights-based?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | How many people can we reach quickly? | Not quite. Reach matters, but HRBA also asks about rights, exclusion, participation, and responsibility. |
| B | Which activity will be easiest to report? | Not quite. Reporting matters, but HRBA should not begin only from what is easiest to report. |
| C | Who is affected, who may be excluded, and who has responsibility to respond? | That’s right. This question connects affected groups, exclusion, and responsibility. |
| D | How can we avoid changing our existing plan? | Not quite. HRBA may require adapting plans when people are excluded or accountability is weak. |

Best response: C

### Question 3

Who are rights-holders?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | Only people who work in human rights organizations | Not quite. All people have rights, not only those working in human rights organizations. |
| B | People and groups who have rights | That’s right. Rights-holders are people and groups who have rights. |
| C | Only government officials | Not quite. Government actors are usually duty-bearers, not the only rights-holders. |
| D | Only people selected for a project activity | Not quite. People do not become rights-holders only when selected for a project. |

Best response: B

### Question 4

What is usually the CSO role in an HRBA?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | To replace all duty-bearers | Not quite. CSOs usually do not replace duty-bearers. They can support rights-holders and strengthen accountability. |
| B | To support participation, evidence, dialogue, and accountability safely | That’s right. CSOs can support participation, evidence, dialogue, and accountability in safe and practical ways. |
| C | To avoid questions about exclusion and responsibility | Not quite. HRBA asks CSOs to notice exclusion and clarify responsibility. |
| D | To collect as much personal data as possible | Not quite. HRBA requires safe and responsible data use, not excessive data collection. |

Best response: B

### Question 5

How should you use the HRBA self-assessment?

| Option | Text | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| A | As a public ranking of CSOs | Not quite. The self-assessment is private and should not be used to rank CSOs. |
| B | As a private learning tool to identify strengths and improvement priorities | That’s right. The self-assessment helps you identify strengths, gaps, and learning priorities. |
| C | As a replacement for community participation | Not quite. Self-assessment does not replace meaningful participation with rights-holders. |
| D | As proof that your CSO already applies HRBA perfectly | Not quite. The self-assessment is a starting point for learning, not proof of perfection. |

Best response: B
