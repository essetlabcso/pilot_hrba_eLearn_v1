# Module 1 HRBA Starting-Point Self-Assessment Survey

## Purpose

The self-assessment helps participants privately reflect on how far their organization, team, or project is already applying HRBA in practice. It is not an audit, not a ranking, and not a certificate test.

## Placement

Section 6 — after learners have already learned:

1. why HRBA matters;
2. the shift from beneficiaries to rights-holders;
3. charity, needs-based, service-delivery, and rights-based practice;
4. rights-holders, duty-bearers, and the CSO role.

## Privacy text

This result is private. It is not a ranking. It does not affect your certificate.

Please answer using broad organizational, team, or project-level judgement. Do not enter names, real case details, complaint records, safeguarding information, confidential documents, or politically sensitive information.

## Response scale

| Score | Response option | Descriptor |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Not yet in place | We do not have this in practice, or only in isolated cases. |
| 1 | Starting | We have discussed it or tried it informally, but it is not consistent. |
| 2 | Partly in place | We do this in some projects or teams, but not yet across the board. |
| 3 | Consistently in place | We do this regularly and can show examples or evidence. |
| N/A | Not sure / need to check | Choose this when you do not have enough information. This option is not scored. |

## Survey items

| Code | Domain | Survey statement |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Participation | People and communities affected by our work are involved early enough to shape plans, not only comment after key decisions are already made. |
| Q2 | Participation | We reduce practical barriers to participation, such as language, disability access, meeting times, transport cost, child care, safety, or fear of speaking. |
| Q3 | Non-discrimination and equality | We actively identify which groups may be excluded or face discrimination in our work, using disaggregated information where possible. |
| Q4 | Non-discrimination and equality | When we see that some groups benefit less, we adjust our activities, outreach, or support rather than treating everyone in the same way. |
| Q5 | Accountability | People know where and how to raise questions, concerns, or complaints about our work, and can do so safely. |
| Q6 | Accountability | We record complaints or feedback, respond within an agreed timeframe, and explain what action was taken. |
| Q7 | Transparency | We explain our objectives, partner roles, selection criteria, and key decisions in plain language people can understand. |
| Q8 | Transparency | We share progress, results, and key changes made because of feedback, including what did not work as planned. |
| Q9 | Empowerment | Our work helps people understand relevant rights, entitlements, and available services or decision-making spaces. |
| Q10 | Empowerment | We strengthen communities, partners, or member groups to speak for themselves and engage decision-makers directly, not only through our organization. |
| Q11 | Legality and rule of law | We check that our activities align with national law and policy while also watching for risks that law or practice may conflict with human rights standards. |
| Q12 | Legality and standards | When relevant, we refer to independent human rights recommendations or reviews, such as treaty body observations, UPR recommendations, or recommendations from national human rights institutions. |
| Q13 | Human rights standards | Our plans clearly link activities to specific rights or human rights standards, not only to general needs or service gaps. |
| Q14 | Human rights standards | We check whether services, information, or support are available, accessible, acceptable, and of reasonable quality for different groups. |
| Q15 | Capacity to integrate HRBA | Staff and key partners have practical guidance, examples, or training they can use to apply HRBA in day-to-day work. |
| Q16 | Capacity to integrate HRBA | HRBA is built into planning, budgeting, monitoring, and review, rather than added at the end as a separate checklist. |

## Scoring logic

```text
total_score = sum numeric responses only
not_sure_count = count N/A
domain_score = sum numeric values for each domain

IF not_sure_count > 4:
  confidence_flag = "low confidence"
ELSE:
  confidence_flag = "standard"

0–15 = Starting point
16–27 = Emerging practice
28–39 = Progressing
40–48 = Embedding HRBA
```

## Overall feedback bands

| Score range | Result band | Automated feedback |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–15 | Starting point | Your organization is at an early stage of applying HRBA. This is a useful baseline. Focus first on participation, non-discrimination, and basic accountability mechanisms before expanding to more advanced integration. |
| 16–27 | Emerging practice | Your organization shows important HRBA building blocks, but practice is not yet consistent. Prioritize formalizing what already works and making it routine across projects and teams. |
| 28–39 | Progressing | Your organization is applying HRBA in a meaningful way. The next step is to improve consistency, evidence, and feedback loops so HRBA is embedded across the program cycle. |
| 40–48 | Embedding HRBA | Your organization shows strong HRBA practice. Maintain momentum by documenting examples, coaching partners, and checking whether good policies are matched by inclusive practice and accountability in reality. |

## Low-confidence feedback

Show if `not_sure_count > 4`:

Your result is useful as a starting point, but confidence is low. Consider repeating the assessment with a colleague who knows program design, MEAL, and accountability systems.

## Domain feedback levels

| Domain score | Label | Generic feedback |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–1 | Needs attention | This area may need focused support. Start with one simple practice you can apply in the next project or activity cycle. |
| 2–3 | Emerging | Some practice exists, but it may be uneven. Make the practice clearer, more routine, and easier for colleagues to apply. |
| 4–5 | Developing well | This is becoming a strength. Focus on consistency, documentation, and learning from what works. |
| 6 | Strong practice | This appears to be a strong area. Keep checking who may still be excluded and whether the practice works in reality. |

## Domain-specific suggestions

| Domain | Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Participation | Start involving affected groups before major decisions are made. Add a simple participation checklist to planning. |
| Non-discrimination and equality | Identify who may be excluded, facing barriers, or benefiting less. Adjust outreach and support accordingly. |
| Accountability | Make feedback and complaints channels clearer, safer, and more trusted. Track whether responses are completed. |
| Transparency | Share objectives, criteria, decisions, and progress in plain language. Close the feedback loop with communities. |
| Empowerment | Help communities understand rights, services, and decision spaces. Create opportunities for groups to speak directly. |
| Legality and rule of law | Add a simple legal and policy check and use relevant human rights recommendations where appropriate. |
| Human rights standards | Link activities to relevant rights or standards without unnecessary jargon. Check access, acceptability, and quality. |
| Capacity to integrate HRBA | Provide practical guidance, examples, refreshers, and integrate HRBA into planning, budgeting, monitoring, and review. |

## Priority selection

Options:

- Transparency

System may suggest the two lowest-scoring domains, but learner can choose different domains.
